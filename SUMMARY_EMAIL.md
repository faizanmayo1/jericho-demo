Hi Benjamin,

Thanks for asking, and fair question. Here it is on one page.

**What we heard**

Three things. Coordinating care across campuses. Catching a change in a resident's condition early
enough to act on it. Keeping staff and families informed without someone having to chase it.

**The one idea**

The residents who become emergencies are usually visible weeks earlier. The signals are already
documented. They are just spread across different logs and written by people who are not clinicians.
A refused evening dose. Dining room attendance dropping from daily to twice a week. A care aide
recording standby assist for a transfer that was independent last month. An activities sheet going
blank. On its own not one of those crosses a threshold, and nothing in a rules engine fires. Read
together across three weeks, it is a pattern.

Assisted living is where this costs the most, because the record is thin by design and the resident
is one bad week away from a transfer nobody saw coming.

**What the layer does**

It reads what your teams already document and connects it across assessments, care plans, medication
records, schedules and incident reports.

It puts a short ranked list in front of your morning stand up, with the reason each name is on it and
the specific notes it came from. Names route to whoever actually owns them, which on the skilled side
is your DON and on the assisted living side is not.

It drafts the family update on the same event, held for a named person to approve. Nothing sends
itself.

**What it would need from you**

Read access to what is already in your EHR and eMAR. No new forms, no second system, nothing extra
for floor staff to fill in. That one is not negotiable on our side either, because anything that adds
documentation to a CNA's shift gets abandoned in a month.

**What you would see on the call**

One view across all your campuses, ranked by what is waiting on somebody rather than by a quality
score, because a league table between your own buildings is a conversation to have with a regional
director and not a number to put on a screen.

Then the morning stand up report the way your buildings already run it. The watch list, and why it
is nine names rather than forty. One resident followed across nineteen days of signal that nobody
had joined up, with the rule each entry sat underneath printed next to it. One incident followed all
the way to closed, with the audit history. The family note being drafted.

The copilot refusing to guess a diagnosis and handing the decision to a named clinician, which is
honestly the part I would most like your read on.

And a straight answer on which part we would test first, which is the assisted living side of one
campus for one quarter, along with what would count as it having failed.

**What it is not**

Not a replacement for your EHR, and not clinical decision making. It prioritizes and it
communicates. Your clinicians decide.

Twenty five minutes covers it. Would Thursday or Friday work, or name a time that suits you better
and I will make it work.
